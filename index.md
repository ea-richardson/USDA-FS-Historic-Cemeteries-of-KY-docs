# Kentucky Historic Cemetery Mapping Project

This project supports the systematic collection and attribution of historic cemeteries throughout Kentucky using 1950s U.S. Geological Survey (USGS) topographic maps and other historical and archaeological records.

The project is administered by the U.S. Forest Service’s Daniel Boone National Forest, Redbird Ranger District, and is conducted in collaboration with the Kentucky Heritage Council and the Commonwealth Office of Technology’s Division of Geographic Information.

Work is organized by county, with research findings and spatial data reviewed before integration into the statewide master geodatabase.

## Project Principles

- **Consistency:** Apply documented methods across counties.
- **Traceability:** Preserve source information and research decisions.
- **Quality:** Review spatial and attribute data before integration.
- **Transparency:** Document uncertainty and conflicting evidence.
- **Accountability:** Maintain current progress and review records.

## Workflow

Each county progresses through five general stages:

1. **Digitization** — Digitize locations from primary sources, including USGS topographic maps.
2. **Research** — Identify cemeteries, match to any existing Find a Grave records, and populate attributes.
3. **Review** — Check completeness, consistency, documentation, and spatial accuracy.
4. **Rework** — Address review findings and document unresolved issues.
5. **Completion and Integration** — Record progress and incorporate reviewed data into the master GDB according to current project procedures.

The county progress tracker records assignments, workflow status, review progress, and outstanding questions (see [Team Access](https://github.com/ea-richardson/USDA-FS-Historic-Cemeteries-of-KY)).

## Data Quality and Documentation

Project data should follow current standards and documented procedures. Contributors are responsible for maintaining accurate attributes, recording supporting sources, identifying data-quality issues, and documenting conflicting evidence or uncertainty.

The presence of a cemetery candidate in project records does not necessarily mean its location or associated information has been independently verified.

## Development Status

Project methods, data structures, metadata standards, and workflows may evolve as research and development continue. Documentation will be updated as project standards and approved procedures evolve.
